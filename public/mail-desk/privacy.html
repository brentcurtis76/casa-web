Privacy policy

Mail Desk uses Google mail data to display your inboxes and conversations, keep a local cache, and make saved mail readable offline.

Updated 14 October 2026 · Current read-only prototype

Mail is cached locally, but the mail database is not encrypted by this prototype. Signing out removes the app’s local account tokens; it does not delete cached messages or revoke Google’s grant.

Who operates Mail Desk

Mail Desk is a personal desktop application maintained by Brent Curtis. This policy describes the current read-only Gmail prototype and this informational website. Questions about privacy or data removal can be sent to brentcurtis76@gmail.com.

What the app can access

When you choose to connect an account, Google’s consent flow requests only https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly. That grant permits reading mail and settings; it does not grant sending or mailbox-modification access. You enter your Google credentials in Google’s system-browser flow, not in Mail Desk.

The app reads your account email address, Gmail labels, message and thread identifiers, dates, headers such as subject/sender/recipient, snippets, label membership, and synchronization information. When a body is explicitly downloaded, its raw message data can include the message text, HTML, inline images, and attachments contained in that message.

The initial cache includes mail from 30 days before that account’s first connection and newer arrivals, excluding Spam, Trash, Draft, and Chat. That cutoff stays fixed. This is an app-side cache filter, not a limit on the Google permission. Synchronization checks can encounter identifiers and current metadata for messages outside the cache scope to decide what should be retained.

Why the app uses this information

Account information keeps accounts separate. Message data supports mailbox lists, conversation reading, cached search, and offline access. Labels and synchronization information keep the local view up to date with changes made in Gmail elsewhere.

Mail Desk does not use mail data for advertising, sell it, or use it to train AI models. The current Gmail integration does not send, modify, archive, trash, or delete mail on Google’s servers. Calendar access is not part of this version.

Where data is stored and transmitted

Google requests use HTTPS. Mail Desk’s Gmail provider exchanges authorization codes and tokens with Google and retrieves mail data from the Gmail API. Google processes those requests under its own policies. The browser’s OAuth callback uses a temporary listener on the local computer.

Messages, downloaded bodies and attachments contained within them, account identifiers, labels, search/selection state, and synchronization checkpoints are stored in a local SQLite cache on the computer running Mail Desk. The initial real-account cache is planned for a separate user-private directory. The prototype does not encrypt that database; people or software with sufficient access to the computer, or copies of its files and backups, may be able to read it.

Access and refresh tokens are stored separately in the existing desktop Secret Service credential collection using an encrypted session. The app stops when that service is unavailable or locked and has no plaintext-token fallback. The current application has no Mail Desk cloud-mail storage, third-party analytics, advertising integration, or automatic mail-data upload.

Diagnostics and support

The native interface redacts message, account, selection, and search payloads from its real-cache diagnostic output. The separate operator helper can print account identifiers, cache-coverage counts, retry information, and errors. Local diagnostic files are not automatically sent to the maintainer.

If you send a support email or choose to share diagnostics, the maintainer receives what you include. Send only the information needed for the issue; omit client configuration files, tokens, authorization codes, and private message content. Any inspection of private messages for support requires your affirmative permission for the specific information.

Retention, disconnection, and removal

Cached data remains on the computer until removed locally or removed by synchronization when a message no longer belongs in the declared cache scope. There is no automatic age-based purge: the initial cutoff stays fixed and the cache can grow. Backups or manually copied files may retain separate copies.

Signing out deletes only the local credentials for that client and account. Cached mail stays readable, and another connected account is preserved. To withdraw Google authorization, use Google Account third-party connections and remove Mail Desk’s access. Removing a grant and removing the local cache are separate steps.

To erase local mail, close the application and helper and remove the appropriate cache and associated database files. A cache may contain multiple accounts, so deleting it can remove all of their cached mail. Keep required information before removal, and remove any copies or backups separately. Contact the maintainer if you need help identifying the files. No deletion is carried out just by visiting this website.

Google data commitments and changes

Mail Desk follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use restrictions, for Google API data. That data is used for the app’s disclosed mail features and is not shared with unrelated services. Any future data access, sharing, or use outside this policy will require an updated disclosure and appropriate consent before it begins.

The update date will change when this policy changes. This policy describes a prototype whose live account behavior is still being validated; it does not claim Google verification or an independent security audit.
