**Three properties of `allocateAll` that decide the shape of your wiring:**

1. **It moves guests.** `allocateAll` runs `balanceMainDishCarriers` first, a bounded
   local search that swaps guests between tables to cover main-dish quotas. Every swap
   preserves each table's people count exactly (G3), respects `maxGuestUnits` (G4),
   never leaves the donor in deficit (G5), and the global guest set is conserved with
   no duplicates and no losses (G9). **`AllocationResult.tables[i].guests` is therefore
   the seating that must be persisted — not the one `planSeating` produced.**
2. **It consumes `pick`.** Per table, in this order and only this order: zero or more
   draws from the willing-guest pool, then exactly one draw for the side-rotation
   offset. Tables are consumed in the order you pass them.
3. **It never produces `"none"`.** Every carrier gets the main dish or a side.

## 4. THE TWO WAYS THIS PHASE GOES WRONG

Named up front, because in P3b naming the real failure modes in advance is what made it
the cheapest phase of the plan.

**Failure mode 1 — food and seating come from different objects.** `allocateAll`
rebalances. If you write `food_assignment` from the allocation but
`guest_participant_id` / `guest_count` from the pre-rebalance `hostStatus`, every count
in every test still adds up and the result is silently wrong: a guest is told to bring
the salad to a dinner they are not sitting at. The write path must consume **one**
object, post-rebalance. Criterion E3 exists for exactly this, and tests 12 and 13 are
what prove it. If you find yourself reading `host.assignedGuests` and an allocation in
the same loop iteration, stop and rethink the shape.

**Failure mode 2 — leftover `pick` consumers.** The two `shuffle([...foodAssignments],
pick)` calls must be **deleted**, not left alongside the allocator. If they survive,
they keep drawing from `pick`, the goldens become unreadable, and E1 ("la comida
proviene **solo** de `allocateAll`") is false even though the food looks right. The
grep in §3.1 catches this.
